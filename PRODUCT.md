# Hallpass

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Attendees first; managers and event organizers second. The initial design walkthrough centers on a first-time attendee who has a concrete work problem and feels overwhelmed by the conference.

## Product Purpose

Help people decide why they are attending, plan around that purpose, adjust during the conference, and understand what they got out of it. Conference goals persist across days.

## Positioning

“Why are you here?” is the central question. Hallpass connects the attendee's purpose to session choices and an evidence-based conference readout.

## Operating Context

A mobile-first experience inside an event platform, which supplies identity. People use it before attending and while navigating a busy venue. The hackathon demonstration uses KubeCon; demo time advancement must be explicit.

Deliver a robust hackathon proof of concept with an end-to-end experience. The submission is a hosted video under three minutes, with narration available. Design the walkthrough for an attendee interested in platform engineering and AI inference, medium energy, and avoiding vendor pitches, following the event plan. The video should show actual working capabilities; prepared data and time advancement must be labeled.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Interview, editable goals, case for attending, day-by-day plan with reasons, next-session guidance, directions, check-ins, re-planning, conference readout, and aggregate organizer insights. Cached plans and directions remain readable offline. No native app, account flow, transcripts, live indoor navigation, or attendee matchmaking.

Current work is UX flows and wireframes, followed later by visual design. No application code is authorized. The user explicitly permits challenging the spec's UX choices. Proposed changes remain labeled proposals until reviewed.

## Brand Commitments

Name: Hallpass. Line: “your pass to the conference you actually wanted.” Short, direct language; no hype, pep talks, or mascot voice. Quiet by default.

Intake must support both direct, focused users and users who want to engage and explore. A sufficiently detailed initial answer can go straight to an editable summary and plan; conversation is optional, never a prerequisite. Exploration uses relevant follow-ups, concise reflections, and easy answer choices. Keep it easy and engaging without being corny: avoid forced humor, praise after every answer, gamification, and artificial suspense.

## Product Principles

- Preserve the attendee's purpose through the entire experience.
- Explain recommendations using the attendee's goals and available session evidence.
- Reduce decisions when attention is scarce while preserving control.
- Describe outcomes honestly; missing feedback is not failure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Text-first participation, optional access needs, keyboard support, readable contrast, labeled controls, visible focus, screen-reader-usable directions, pacing and breaks. Voice is never required. Organizer insights exclude personal notes and access needs.

## Open Decisions

Review approval of plan changes, persistent navigation, and check-in structure in the UX draft. Adaptive intake supporting both direct input and optional exploration is confirmed; its detailed wording remains to be tested. Visual identity is deliberately deferred.
