## The idea

Scott suggested a “HELLO my name is Hall Pass” sticker badge. This exploration uses that recognizable conference introduction as the identity, with the existing product spelling “Hallpass.” The spacing of the name remains a branding decision for review.

The emotional aim: an approachable introduction to a conference that starts with you. Personality is concentrated in the first-use logo and small, useful moments during the day. The schedule remains a clear working interface.

## What changes from the first direction

- A vermilion-and-white HELLO sticker replaces the large plain wordmark on entry. A readable marker-style name provides character without making body copy handwritten.
- Compact operational headers use a simpler Hallpass wordmark on vermilion; the full badge's small wording should not be squeezed into tiny spaces.
- Warm ivory, ink navy, and cobalt actions make the interface more expressive. Pale butter identifies a planned break, and pale blue distinguishes a suggested replacement.
- “Room to breathe” gives the break a humane voice while the explicit duration remains visible. No celebration for ordinary clicks.
- Current and proposed sessions stay aligned by time; their labels and position explain the comparison independently of color.

## What stays

Direct and exploratory intake, clear primary actions, goal-linked reasons, explicit review of plan changes, visible breaks, and familiar navigation. No mascots, points, confetti, forced jokes, or extra steps introduced for personality.

## Production implications, after approval

The generated board is a design reference, not a reusable logo source. A final logo needs a clean vector master, a compact mark, and single-color variants. Check the badge name at small sizes; reserve the full HELLO wording for sizes where it can be read. Sticker rotation and corner detail must not affect input or control alignment.

Use flat colors in implementation. Verify text contrast, focus states, large text, touch targets, and reduced motion with the real interface. Avoid moving or peeling interactions that delay a task. Real session content must replace the labeled illustrative examples before recording.
